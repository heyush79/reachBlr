How the times are worked out

Metro. Ride times and the gap between trains come from the Namma Metro weekday timetable (BMRCL GTFS, version of 17 August 2026, from the unofficial bmrcl-gtfs project). Waiting time is half the gap between trains at that station. Getting from the street to the platform adds 2 minutes, leaving adds 1 minute, and changing lines adds 3 minutes plus the wait. Only lines open in the timetable (Purple, Green, Yellow) are included.

Bus. Routes, stops and ride times come from the BMTC weekday timetable (version of 9 November 2026, from the unofficial bmtc-gtfs project, built from the Namma BMTC app; it only includes routes with live tracking). The timetable assumes about 22 km/h at every hour, but news reports put real bus speeds at 10 to 12 km/h in peak traffic, so bus rides here take 1.8 times the timetable time in the morning and 1.4 times in the evening. When several routes go from your stop to the same next stop, their buses are counted together for the wait. The wait is half the gap between buses, at least 1 minute and at most 15 minutes, since riders can check live bus times in the app. Morning counts buses from 7 to 11 am, evening from 7:30 to 10 pm. Changing between buses, or between bus and metro, means walking up to 250 m between bus stops or 400 m to a metro station.

Getting there. Walking is 4.8 km/h along a path 30% longer than a straight line. An auto or cab is 18 km/h in traffic plus 5 minutes to get one. A ward counts as reachable if you can get to its nearest part, plus a 300 m walk into it, so the far side of a large ward will take longer. Times are from your office outward; the morning trip to work is usually close to this. Office locations are approximate.

What's nearby. Gyms, parks, hospitals and clinics, schools, grocery shops, pharmacies and places to eat come from OpenStreetMap (data as of , © OpenStreetMap contributors). The map is built by volunteers and misses many places, especially small shops and gyms, so treat counts as a guide, not a full list. Each ward shows how many of each are inside it and how far the nearest one is from the ward centre. A ward's score for each type compares how many it has per square km with all 243 wards (100 means more than almost every other ward, 0 means none mapped). The everyday needs score is the average of the seven. "Must have within 1 km" keeps a ward if it has at least one inside, or one within 1 km of its centre.
